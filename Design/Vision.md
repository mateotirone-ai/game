# Vision

**Aevum** is a persistent multiplayer civilization game experienced through a **phoenix settler** — you can die and return, while the **city grows around you forever**. Players survive, build, specialize, trade, govern, cooperate, and conflict as settlements grow from tiny homesteads into large cities inside a living, procedural world.

## Elevator pitch

You are not a disposable life in a reset loop. You are a lasting agent in history: founding hearths, shaping industries, writing laws, brokering peace, or sparking wars—while the settlement continues whether you are online or not. Death is real; the civilization is what endures.

## Experience fantasy

- **Phoenix continuity** — You can die (gravestones, setbacks). You return. Reputation, property, and the settlement persist across sessions and eras.
- **Civilization from the ground up** — Start with survival and shelter; end with specialized districts, institutions, and regional power.
- **Real economics** — Goods have costs, scarcity, logistics, and opportunity cost—not abstract victory points alone.
- **Social systems as gameplay** — Democracy, diplomacy, businesses, and cooperation are first-class, not flavor text.
- **Stylized clarity** — Soft stylized readability, strong color language, and controller-first UI over photoreal grit.

## Non-goals (for early milestones)

- Photoreal graphics
- Full planetary MMO scale on day one
- Offline-only single-player as the long-term architecture
- Pay-to-win monetization of power (buy-once intent)
- Unkillable “immortal god” avatar fantasy

## Success look

A session where a player gathers, builds a workshop, sells surplus into a regional market, votes on a road levy, and sees a neighboring settlement specialize in ore—then negotiates a trade pact—while understanding that their hearth can outlast any one death.
